# W12.4 outcome

The obligation-driven controller passed all four gates in all three repetitions.
On the ten challenge cases, weighted closure was 90%, compared with 0% for fixed
retrieval and 0% for the generic W10 diagnostic replay. The remaining open case
was intentionally unanswerable. Treatment left one importance-3 obligation open,
versus ten for each comparison arm, admitted no low-value source, and spent one of
22 queries after the last material gain (4.5%). Both easy cases stopped after the
initial query.

This is strong evidence for the control contract and weak evidence for product
effect size. Candidate availability and gold closure relations were constructed
to expose policy behavior. The result proves that a named obligation can govern
proposal selection, evidence admission, and stopping without a loop. It does not
prove that a model can author accurate obligations or that live retrieval will
produce the same 90-point difference.

The appropriate next step is a bounded experimental path for caller-supplied or
independently validated obligations. Fixed retrieval remains the stable default.
A live calibration must measure obligation-state accuracy, retrieval yield, and
cost before promotion.
