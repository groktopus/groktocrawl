# W12.3 independent claim-verification experiment brief

## Decision

Determine whether one blinded, independent semantic verification step should be
added before publication for high-risk or contradiction-bearing claims.

## User-visible problem

A structurally valid claim can still be wrong. It may cite a source that does not
entail it, ignore a contradiction, rely on stale or derivative evidence, confuse
two similarly named subjects, or obey instructions embedded in source text. W12.3
tests whether an independent verifier catches enough of those errors to justify
its delay and tendency to abstain.

## Independence boundary

The cases and expected judgments are authored and sealed before model execution.
The control decision uses the existing source-bound structural eligibility
recorded in each case. The treatment adds a blinded verifier through the thin
local LiteLLM `general` route. The claims are fixed rather than generated by that
route, so the verifier is independent of claim generation and control selection.
It receives only the claim, exact evidence spans, risk, and evidence obligation.
It receives no control decision, expected judgment, generator rationale, arm
identity, or publication preference.

Scoring is deterministic against the sealed references. The verifier never grades
itself and cannot publish, authorize, rewrite, or claim human approval.

## Durable artifacts

- the typed boundary and fail-closed validators;
- frozen claim corpus, references, work order, prompts, and route identity;
- raw verifier outputs, receipts, failures, and parsed records;
- repetition-level risk and calibration analysis;
- secret-free evidence packet, outcome, and proposed ADR.
